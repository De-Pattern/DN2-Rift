# v0.28 · STABLE

**RIFT** is a second firmware for the Digitone II, separate from the DN2 mk2 mod. v0.28 is a stability release: no new machines, the reports from the bug channel worked through.

**Build it:** the DN2 Mod Patcher, its RIFT section: https://de-pattern.github.io/dn2mk2/patcher.html#rift. It builds RIFT in your browser from your own Digitone II OS 1.11, Digitakt II OS 1.17 and Machinedrum UW OS 1.63 files, and only lets you save it if it is byte for byte the cleared file. The **MANUAL** and **QUICK GUIDE** links sit beside the version list.

**⚠ Before you flash**
- **RIFT is new and early.** It passed the full ship gate (both processors' emulators, the DSP checks and the unit's cost model, boot, save and load), but it has had very little time on real instruments yet.
- **Back up** the +Drive and your projects with Elektron Transfer first, every time. Keep the stock OS 1.11 file and a DIN MIDI cable for recovery. The start-up code and the updater are Elektron's own, untouched.
- **Coming from the DN2 mk2 mod:** flash it like any mod version. Tracks with **Perc** or the **Monomachine** machines open as **FM Tone** in RIFT: their sounds are not kept. Save copies of projects you care about.
- **Going back:** RIFT's machines (FASCIA, ORGAN, SILT, LIGATURE, DIGIDRUM, CONTOUR, SMEAR, SPALL) and the plate reverb will not sound the same, or at all, on the mod or stock. Keep RIFT projects apart.
- OS 1.11 only.

**New in v0.28**
- **RUBBER is pulled.** It made voices go quiet after a few notes, crackled on FM Tone and could drop out even in an empty project (thanks franny, InductiveOne). A sound saved with it loads as Multi-Mode.
- **The sample machines and SMEAR hold up under load.** The DSP's time guard asked every sample voice for about twice what it costs, on every block, so with a few tracks playing, voices dropped out with time to spare (thanks lumbre, franny). It now works as v0.27's fix for RIFT's machines: a voice is expected to cost what it cost last block, and more only on the block its note starts.
- **SMEAR no longer cuts out after 30 to 50 seconds.** The time guard could stop SMEAR's own sample glue and then leave it stopped; that glue now always runs, and SMEAR's grains get the time a GRN note really needs on the block it starts (thanks franny).
- **CONTOUR with FDBK turned up mid-note** no longer goes silent: the guard expects the cost of its higher feedback steps on every block, not only on the note's first.
- **The master reverb uses about a third less DSP time**, the same plate bit for bit, and its tail is a little shorter (at DEC 127 about a quarter: 42 s to 32 s; at 96 about a tenth; below that barely).
- **DIGIDRUM:** the bottom row's names follow the drum type in the header and the LFO lists (Sweep, Square Mod...), not "Type Control 1..4" (thanks franny).
- **Smoother pages:** a choice's word on RIFT's pages now scrolls in smoothly, and every redraw of those pages is cheaper.
- **Manual and quick guide:** FLEX REC and the recorder open with [FUNC] + [UNISON], the key that opens VOICE SETUP.
- Includes v0.27 (the CPU marker, CONTOUR's voices, the MIDI arp and in-key transpose).

**What to test**
1. A busy project with sample tracks and SMEAR: fewer drop-outs, the CPU marker rarer; SMEAR held for a minute or more keeps sounding.
2. CONTOUR: raise FDBK while a note is held.
3. The reverb: the same character, a slightly shorter tail at high DEC.
4. DIGIDRUM: turn O1..O4 on each drum type; the header names them.
5. A project that used RUBBER: those tracks now Multi-Mode.
